# Tayyab Shoukat – The Backbone of Japan Electronics

*The Man Behind the Insights: A Story of Loyalty, Intelligence, Leadership and an Unfinished Journey*

> This is my personal, first-person story. Unless a section is labelled otherwise, it is my own recollection and has not been independently verified. My labelled record is on the [employee experience](../experience/) page, my work is listed on the [professional contributions](../contributions/) page, and the company may respond on the [right of reply](../company-response) page.

*Written on 15 November 2026, at 1:22 a.m., from my newly rented home in Islamabad.*

Some people become part of an organisation's history. Others leave behind a story worth remembering.

At an award ceremony, the General Manager of Japan Electronics called me "the Backbone of Japan Electronics".

A few months after I came back to that company, my employment ended.

This is the story of how I came back, why I was given that name, and why I am now writing these words in a house I had prepared for a very different future.

## Why I came back to Islamabad — *My recollection*

Approximately a year and a half after my earlier time with the company, the General Manager contacted me in Karachi and asked me to return to Islamabad.

I did not say yes for a salary alone.

I said yes because of Japan Electronics' reputation. I said yes because of the professional relationship I had with its management. I remembered a workplace where employees voluntarily worked late, where professional relationships felt personal, and where the Director and CEO were addressed as Bhai (brother). We worked with a sense of belonging, and I believed that trust still existed.

I also had a natural affection for Islamabad.

But there was one more reason, the one that mattered more than any designation. I will come to it later.

So I left my job in Karachi, left the city, and returned on 11 May 2026. I rented a new home in Islamabad.

I was not returning as a stranger looking for work. I was returning to an organisation I believed in.

## Loyalty that did not depend on location — *My recollection*

One incident captures my approach particularly well.

I was in Karachi when I noticed that product prices and annual sales information had been missed on the company website. I was no longer physically in Islamabad, but I contacted the Director and General Manager by phone and alerted them to the issue.

I could have ignored it. It was not a matter I needed to handle at that moment.

But I did not see the company's digital information as somebody else's problem. For me, responsibility did not end at the office door, and loyalty did not depend on physical presence.

## Why I was called the Backbone of Japan Electronics

### The recognition — *My recollection; Awaiting documentary verification*

At an award ceremony, the General Manager referred to me as:

> "The Backbone of Japan Electronics."

I also received the Best Team Leader award and certificate.

That recognition remains meaningful to me. It represented the trust and value I believed I had built through creativity, speed, ownership and analytical work.

I also remember a remark from the Director about my working style: that if I was in the right mood, I could complete hours of work in minutes. It was a light-hearted expression, but it captured the confidence and working relationship we had developed.

### Business Intelligence and Strategic Planning — *My recollection*

My department was Business Intelligence. My functional leadership work centred on Business Intelligence, Strategic Planning and Performance Analytics. This describes the work I did, not an officially documented designation.

I wanted to do more than prepare reports. I wanted management to understand what the business numbers actually meant.

My work, in short:

- Business Intelligence
- Strategic Planning
- Business Planning
- Sales Target Intelligence
- Performance Analytics
- Management Insights
- Digital Tool Development

Day to day, that meant management reporting, dashboards and KPI monitoring, business and competitor research, and process improvement and automation.

I was not merely reporting what had happened. I was identifying what the numbers were saying, what the business might be overlooking, and where management needed to look next. I connected information, recognised patterns, highlighted gaps and brought forward insights that could influence business priorities and decisions. My role was not simply to present information; it was to turn information into insight.

I believe this distinction mattered. Reports describe performance, but meaningful Business Intelligence helps management understand why things are happening, what deserves attention and what opportunities or risks may lie ahead.

I tried to connect information from different business areas so that decisions could be made with a clearer understanding of performance. The full list of this work is on the [professional contributions](../contributions/) page, where every item is marked Pending documentary verification.

### From Business Plans to Sales Targets: Driving Performance Intelligence — *My recollection; Awaiting documentary verification*

A target is only useful if someone follows it all the way through. My work covered the whole cycle:

Planning → Target Setting → Follow-up → Achievement → Comparison → Presentation → Management Insights

Within that cycle, I worked on:

- Sales and product targets
- Branch manager and branch performance
- Online team targets
- Marketing targets and budget analysis
- Purchase and business plan targets
- Corporate and wholesale targets
- SDA (Small Domestic Appliances, such as kitchen appliances and beauty and grooming products) and LED targets
- Sales versus target, and achievement monitoring
- Sales per square foot reporting
- Branch-wise and department-wise performance
- Brand-wise and branch-wise NC analysis (NC, or Normal Condition, is an internal classification for products with minor imperfections, such as light scratches, small dents or a cracked button)
- Growth and decline comparisons
- Slow-moving products and inventory analysis
- Target follow-up and performance gaps
- Strategy and competitor comparisons
- Management reporting and presentations

I did not approve every target, and I did not make management's decisions. My part was to build the plans and targets, follow them through, and show management clearly where the business stood and where it was falling short.

### Beyond Reports: Building Business Intelligence Tools — *My recollection; Awaiting documentary verification*

My contributions were not limited to conventional Business Intelligence reporting. Over time, the questions I was answering grew, and so did the way I answered them:

Business Intelligence Reporting → Market Research → Competitor Intelligence → Functional Digital Tools → AI-Assisted Development

Reporting showed management what was happening inside the business. Market research and competitor intelligence showed what was happening around it. The next step was to turn that analysis into tools that could be used again and again.

- **Market research tools:** practical tools for researching markets, identifying business opportunities and analysing market trends.
- **Competitor analysis tools:** functional tools for examining competitors, comparing their market presence and supporting strategic business decisions.
- **Vibe coding and AI-assisted development:** I practised vibe coding and used AI-assisted development to turn ideas into working digital tools and applications.

These were functional tools, not just ideas or theoretical proposals. For me, they were an extension of Business Intelligence: the same discipline of asking the right questions, built into something the business could use.

### A product website with more than 3,000 products — *My recollection; Awaiting documentary verification*

Another significant contribution was my work on the Japan Electronics product website, containing more than 3,000 products.

According to my account, I was the first person to build that product website. I saw it as an opportunity to bring product information into a structured digital presence and to contribute beyond traditional reporting responsibilities.

### Delivering, whatever the circumstances — *My recollection*

There were occasions when I submitted reports while travelling in the company van. During my hostel stay, I also worked late at night to complete management requests.

The work was demanding, but I took pride in being dependable.

### More than reports: what my work added — *My recollection*

Japan Electronics was built by many people and many departments. Its business position, operations and digital development were never the work of one person, and I do not claim otherwise.

But I know what my part was.

I helped turn business data into management intelligence. My reporting and performance analytics brought greater visibility to how the business was really performing. My dashboards, KPI monitoring and strategic planning work gave management clearer ground for its decisions. My market research and competitor analysis tools looked beyond the company's own numbers, to the market it was competing in. My digital and AI-assisted tools were an effort to build practical solutions, not only to point out problems. And my work on the product website, with more than 3,000 products, was an important part of the company's digital presence.

If Japan Electronics has reached greater heights, I am proud that my work was part of that journey. I did not build it alone, and I never will claim that I did. But I gave it my thinking, my analysis, my creativity, my time and my dedication. My contribution was never only in the reports I submitted. It was in the insights I generated, the tools I built, the opportunities I identified and the decisions I helped to inform.

## The contrast: from trust to uncertainty — *My recollection; Disputed*

I came back because of trust. After rejoining in May 2026, I gradually felt that the working environment had changed from the one I remembered.

In my observation, some employees appeared more cautious and concerned about job security. I also heard concerns from former colleagues.

I began examining policies and incentive structures, particularly in After-Sales. I questioned whether the decisions were sufficiently supported by departmental facts, performance figures and operational research. My concern was that policies affecting employees should be designed after understanding the actual work and the people expected to follow them.

In October 2026, salary timing became an important concern that I raised with management. The salary cycle ran from the 26th to the 25th. According to my account, the salary for the September cycle was received on 13 October 2026. The delayed October salary was a separate concern, which I also raised with management. I thought about the impact on employees, including female staff living in hostels, who could face difficulties arranging food, accommodation and everyday expenses.

I specifically referred to a colleague's incentive concern in my email to management. I also described incidents that I believed reflected growing frustration, including a WhatsApp voice message incident and an employee who, according to my recollection, managed lunch with only a samosa and biscuits. To me, these examples showed why salary timing, incentive accuracy, employee trust and word of mouth deserved management attention.

On 24 October 2026, according to my recollection, I sent approximately four to five emails raising organisational concerns and proposing solutions. One of those emails was followed by a specific meeting with the General Manager, where I discussed the delayed October salary, its impact on employees and its possible effect on morale and word of mouth.

Even on the day my employment ended, I was trying to communicate issues that I believed could be improved.

I cannot establish that these concerns had any connection with the employment decision. My labelled account of the employment itself, including the PIP / warning document I refused to sign and the final-settlement process, is on the [employee experience](../experience/) page.

## What the company said — *Company's written response; Disputed*

My employment ended on 24 October 2026.

In its written response dated 12 November 2026, the company attributed the decision to restructuring and cost optimisation, stated that the decision was final and said that my dues had been settled and cleared.

I dispute that all my employment-related and settlement concerns were resolved.

I do not claim to know the reasons behind every management decision, and I do not claim that my reporting caused my separation. ## The home I had prepared — *My recollection*

This is the reason I held back.

I had rented a new home in Islamabad with the intention of bringing my mother there, so that I could have her with me and look after her. A stable position with a company I trusted, in a city I loved, seemed to make that possible.

That was why returning from Karachi had meant so much to me.

Now, at 1:22 in the morning, I am writing this story from that same home, reflecting on a future that did not unfold as I had planned.

## What I learned

Japan Electronics taught me about business operations, performance analysis, leadership, digital platforms, teamwork and the importance of translating information into practical decisions.

I learned that Business Intelligence is not merely about numbers. It is about asking the right questions, understanding the people behind the figures and having the courage to communicate what the evidence suggests.

I am proud of my contribution. I am grateful for the recognition and the lessons. I also know that no individual builds an organisation alone.

## The next chapter

Karachi was where I received the call.

Islamabad was where I returned, worked, rented a home and imagined looking after my mother.

The title "Backbone of Japan Electronics" was given to me by the General Manager. The work behind that recognition, the loyalty I demonstrated and the lessons I earned will remain part of my own professional history.

So I will leave you with the question I keep asking myself:

> The man once called the Backbone of Japan Electronics — where is he now, and what is he building next?

The Backbone is no longer inside the building. But his story is still being written.

Will his next destination be Karachi, Islamabad, or somewhere no one has guessed yet?
